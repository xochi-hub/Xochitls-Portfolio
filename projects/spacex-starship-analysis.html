Analysis of SpaceX Starship Model

  • Spring 2024
  • Aerodynamics
  • Wind Tunnel Testing

For ME 103, Experimentation and Measurements, my team of three tested a 1:200 scale Starship model in the wind tunnel in UC Berkeley’s Hesse Hall and compared the results with computational fluid dynamics (CFD) simulations. We measured drag at three angles of attack and three wind speeds. The project compared how well simulation software predicts real aerodynamic forces.

  • Designed a 3D-printed resin mount to hold the model at 0°, 5°, and 10° angles of attack, with a 45.8 mm inner bore and a lofted connecting rod tapped with 1/4-20 threads that screws directly onto the wind tunnel’s strain gauge rod
  • Calibrated the strain gauge with hanging masses from 1 to 100 g, rotating it to its most sensitive orientation and building a linear force-to-voltage relationship in MATLAB to turn raw readings into drag force
  • Collected 135 steady-state readings in LabVIEW across 9 speed and angle conditions (10, 16.5, and 22 m/s), keeping force standard deviations at or below 0.01 N
  • Modeled the full test setup in Autodesk CFD to match the wind tunnel’s 10″ × 12″ cross section. 7 of 9 simulated drag coefficients came within 13% of the wind tunnel values. One case was 42% off (0° at 16.5 m/s), which showed how relying on simulation alone could mislead a design decision
