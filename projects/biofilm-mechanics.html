Biofilm Mechanics Blocking Tissue Repair

2026 · Computational Biomechanics · Multiscale MD–FEM Simulation

Bacterial biofilms cause many stubborn infections in open wounds because they are hard to remove both mechanically and chemically. Built for Computational Biomechanics Across Multiple Scales, this project asks how the stiffness of a biofilm physically affects a healing wound. I built a two-stage simulation that connects the molecular scale to the tissue scale. First, a molecular dynamics model of the biofilm’s polymer network (EPS) is stretched and held to find its stiffness and relaxation time. Those values then feed a finite element model of a wound bed, where tissue and biofilm layers are squeezed together to mimic wound contraction. Comparing no biofilm, a soft biofilm, and a stiff biofilm shows how biofilm stiffness changes the stress inside the wound.

  • Built a two-stage multiscale simulation linking molecular dynamics (OpenMM) to finite element modeling (FEniCSx), where material properties from the first stage drive the second
  • Modeled the biofilm’s polymer network as a coarse-grained bead-spring system (8 chains, 200 beads, random crosslinks) and applied tensile and step-strain loading to extract a Young’s modulus of about 3,650 kPa and a relaxation time of 1.74 ps
  • Meshed a two-layer tissue and biofilm wound bed in Gmsh and simulated wound contraction across three conditions: no biofilm, compliant biofilm, and stiff biofilm
  • Found that a stiff biofilm produced about 104× higher peak von Mises stress at the tissue-biofilm interface, while a compliant biofilm behaved almost the same as no biofilm
