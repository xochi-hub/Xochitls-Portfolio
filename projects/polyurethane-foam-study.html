Environmental Degradation of PE-PUR Foam in Respiratory Devices

2024–2026 · Materials Testing · Medical Devices

CPAP and BiPAP machines help people breathe while they sleep, and inside many of them is a layer of polyester polyurethane (PE-PUR) foam that dampens motor noise. In 2021, Philips recalled millions of these devices because the foam could break down and release particles and chemicals into the user’s airway. Researching in the Berkeley Biomechanics Lab under Professor Grace O’Connell, I aged commercial PE-PUR foam for four weeks in three realistic conditions: room temperature, desert-level heat, and high humidity. I then measured how each condition changed the foam’s density, appearance, and strength.

  • Designed a four-week lab-aging study with three conditions: room temperature (control), high temperature (40 to 45 °C) in a convection oven, and high humidity (85 to 95% RH) in a fog room, with four samples per condition
  • Sourced commercial foam matching the recalled devices’ density, then calibrated each environment and tracked mass, density, and visual changes weekly using a custom observation rubric
  • Performed Instron tensile testing following ASTM D3574, adding foam pads at the grips to reduce slipping, and analyzed the stress-strain data in MATLAB
  • Found that high humidity caused a 33% drop in elastic modulus, lower tensile strength, and pink and black spots consistent with microbial growth, while high heat caused only minor stiffening and particle shedding
