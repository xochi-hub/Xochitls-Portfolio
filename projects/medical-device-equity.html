Medical Device Equity in Low-Resource Analysis

2026 · Global Health · Medical Devices

Mexico is one of the largest medical device manufacturers in Latin America, yet many rural and Indigenous communities still go without basic diagnostic tools. This gap has deep roots: by 1938, more than 67% of rural municipalities had no physician. Built for Critical Systems of Development, this research project looks at why medical devices fail to reach and stay working in low-resource communities in Mexico. Through a literature review, I found that the problem is not a lack of technology, but fragmented health systems, weak communication between clinicians and procurement, and devices designed without the communities who use them. My interest in this topic comes from my family, who originated in rural Michoacán before moving to Mexico City.

  • Reviewed 15+ sources across health policy, Mexican health history, procurement, and community co-design to map why devices fail in low-resource settings
  • Created a constellation diagram connecting social, economic, organizational, and infrastructure forces, and a stakeholder map showing who holds power over device decisions
  • Identified the procurement-clinical divide, where devices are often bought based on price instead of patient need because clinicians and purchasing staff don’t share information
  • Proposed a layered strategy combining insurance expansion, knowledge-sharing feedback loops between rural clinics and decision-makers, and community co-design with local health workers (promotoras)
