IoT Cup Pong

  • Fall 2023
  • IoT
  • Embedded Systems

IoT Pong is an automated cup pong game that keeps score on its own and pours a drink for the opposing player when a ball lands in a cup. Built for ME 100, Electronics for the Internet of Things, with a team of three, the game uses three ESP32 microcontrollers that talk to each other wirelessly. Each cup has a laser sensor that detects when the ball breaks the beam, and its ESP32 sends the point to a third ESP32, which updates the score and runs a motor-driven valve to pour 1.5 ounces from a jug. The goal was to show how connected devices can automate a game and keep players focused on playing instead of tracking points.

  • Built a three-ESP32 system that communicates over MQTT, with each cup’s ESP32 publishing score events to its own topic and a third ESP32 subscribing to both to control the pour
  • Replaced the first prototype’s ultrasonic sensor, which was unreliable at short range, with a laser break-beam sensor on each cup to detect when the ball lands
  • Wrote MicroPython code with a 2-second debounce, so a ball bouncing in the cup isn’t counted twice and a point only counts once the ball settles
  • Wired and programmed a DC motor and valve with PWM control to open and close the jug and pour a set amount for each point scored
