Gyroscope Glove

2025 · Assistive Devices · Human-Centered Design

Tremors are involuntary, rhythmic movements that make everyday tasks like writing, eating, and cooking difficult. They vary from person to person and even throughout the day, so there is no one-size-fits-all solution. Existing devices can help, but people we interviewed said they are often bulky, uncomfortable, hard to put on, or hard to clean. Built for Advanced Designing for the Human Body with a team of four, this glove uses a spinning gyroscopic wheel on the back of the hand to resist wrist tremors while keeping the fingers free. The wheel’s speed can be adjusted with a knob, and weights can be added or removed, which let us experiment with how spin and weight affect tremors. We tested different combinations with a user to learn what the design needs to reduce shaking in everyday use.

  • Interviewed people with tremors and ran drawing tests with and without arm support to define what a device needs: comfort, quick on and off, and use during daily tasks
  • Iterated multiple glove designs with adjustable wrist and forearm straps to fit different hands, integrating 3D-printed parts and sewn attachments to hold the gyroscope and weights securely
  • Experimented with wheel speed and added weight across six conditions with a user, using an IMU and MATLAB to measure hand motion
